# Claiming the Google Business Profile — the correct way

> **Read this before doing anything on Google.** The instinctive approach —
> create a new profile, then report the old one — is the one route that reliably
> makes things worse. This document explains why, and what to do instead.

---

## Why not to create a duplicate

The existing profile (Place ID `ChIJHUgV0gQmtRQRsb2D_txDS2Q`) sits at your address
and carries whatever history Google has accumulated about your business since it
first appeared. If you create a second profile for the same location:

1. **Google's duplicate detection catches it, usually within days.** Same address,
   same category, same name — this is the easiest pattern in the world to spot.
2. **The likely outcome is that *your new one* gets suppressed or suspended**, not
   the old one. The older profile has more history and is treated as the incumbent.
3. **You lose the review**, and more importantly the *age signal*. A profile that
   Google has known about for years outranks a profile created last Tuesday, even
   with identical information.
4. **Suspensions are slow and painful to reverse** — reinstatement requests take
   weeks and often need documentary proof. Meanwhile you have no listing at all,
   which is worse than the 2.3★ one you have now.
5. **"Reporting" the old profile won't work.** Google's redressal process is for
   spam, fake listings, and closed businesses — not for "I've lost the password."
   Marking your own real, operating shop as closed or fake is a false report, and
   it can be applied to the listing permanently.

Your problem isn't that the profile is wrong. It's that you don't have the keys.
Google has a specific, designed process for exactly that.

## The correct route: request ownership

Google's [Request ownership of a Business Profile](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4566671?hl=en)
flow exists precisely for the case where a profile is claimed by someone you can't
reach — a former web agency, an ex-partner, an employee who left, a relative who
set it up once and forgot.

**How it works:**

1. Sign in with the Google account you want to own the profile long-term.
   → Use a **business account you control**, not a personal Gmail you might lose
   access to, and not an account tied to a former partner.
2. Find the shop on Google Maps or Search.
3. Click **"Claim this business" / "Bu işletmeyi talep et"** → you'll be told the
   profile is already managed, and offered **"Request access" / "Erişim iste"**.
4. Fill in the form — your name, your relationship to the business, your contact
   details. Be accurate; this becomes evidence if it escalates.
5. Google emails the current owner. **Google will show you a partially masked
   version of their email address** — this alone is often revealing. If it looks
   like a family member's or a former partner's address, you may be able to just
   phone them and ask.
6. **The current owner has 3–7 days to respond.**
   - **No response** → Google offers to transfer the profile to you. You then go
     through standard verification (video, most likely).
   - **They approve** → you get access immediately.
   - **They deny** → you can escalate to Google support with proof of ownership.

## What to have ready

Given the partner split, a denial is possible. Prepare the documentation before
you start, not after:

- **Çerkezköy TSO registration** — the Ltd. Şti. is registered at Pınar Bulvarı 56/A.
  This is the strongest single document you have, because it's an independent
  official record tying your company to this exact address.
- Oda Sicil No **3037** / Ticaret Sicil No **3230**
- A utility bill or rent contract in the business's name at the address
- Vergi levhası
- Photos of the storefront signage
- The domain registration, which dates to 2013 and is in your control

If it escalates, this package makes it straightforward. Google's support staff are
looking for exactly this kind of independent corroboration.

## About the former partner

Worth thinking through before you start, because it affects tone and expectations:

- If the profile was created during the partnership, it may sit on **his** Google
  account. The masked email in step 5 will tell you.
- If so, **the fastest possible resolution is a phone call**, not a Google process.
  He has no use for a profile pointing at your shop's address, and transferring it
  costs him nothing. Ask before escalating — a two-minute conversation beats a
  three-week support case.
- His shop is now under a different name at a different address, so there's no
  ongoing competitive reason for him to hold onto it.
- If the relationship makes that call impossible, the 7-day non-response route
  works fine on its own. You don't need his cooperation, only his silence.

## After you get access

- **Don't rename or re-address it aggressively on day one.** Make changes over a
  week or two. A profile that changes name, address, phone and category within an
  hour of an ownership transfer looks exactly like a hijacking, and can trip a
  manual review.
- **Fix the most damaging things first:** the website URL (once the site is live),
  the phone number, and the map pin.
- **Remove the former partner's phone numbers** — 0282 717 85 88 and 0282 717 39 87
  belong to the other shop and should not appear anywhere on your listing.
- **Then reply to the 2.3★ review.** Calm, specific, no defensiveness. Everyone who
  looks at the profile reads the owner's reply, and it's often worth more than the
  rating itself.
- Add photos, hours, categories, and description over the following days.

## If Google won't release it

Rare, but if the request is denied and support won't help:

- You can file a **Business Redressal Complaint** — but only on legitimate grounds,
  such as the profile listing information that is factually wrong about a business
  you can prove you own.
- Do **not** report it as fake or permanently closed. It isn't, and a false report
  is both a policy violation and the kind of thing that's hard to walk back.
- At that point, get advice before acting. Creating a duplicate remains the wrong
  answer no matter how frustrating the process gets.
